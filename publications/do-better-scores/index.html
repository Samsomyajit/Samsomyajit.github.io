Do Better Scores Mean Better Physics? Physics-Grounded Explanations for Sim2Real Neural Operators

A diagnostic study of when lower neural-operator prediction error can disagree with physically meaningful flow statistics in a Sim2Real airfoil forecasting problem.

Read on arXiv OpenReview Project page

Plain-language summary

Scientific machine-learning models are often compared using one or two aggregate prediction errors. This study asks whether those scores tell the whole story. Using experimental PIV measurements and paired CFD development data for flow around a NACA4418 airfoil, we compare neural-operator predictions with fluctuation-energy diagnostics and controlled perturbation tests.

Main findings

  • Across four tested neural-operator architectures, masking the most energetic regions of the observed flow history changes forecasts more than masking equal-area random regions.
  • A CNO can achieve lower velocity-field error than the reference while producing substantially higher two-component fluctuation-energy error.
  • The results show that aggregate prediction error and physically meaningful flow diagnostics can rank the same forecasts differently.

Interpretation and scope

The masking experiment is interpreted as a model-sensitivity diagnostic rather than a causal measure of physical importance: equal-area energetic and random masks do not remove equal amounts of fluctuation energy, and the modified histories do not have a known physical counterfactual future. The two analysis subsets are window-disjoint but share the same 23 experimental trajectories, so their agreement is replication rather than trajectory-disjoint generalization.
