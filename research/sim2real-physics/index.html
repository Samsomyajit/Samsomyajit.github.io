01 / THE QUESTION

A better score can tell an incomplete story.

Neural operators are commonly compared using aggregate prediction errors. But a velocity field can look closer to measurements while its fluctuation dynamics remain less faithful. This study examines that mismatch in a Sim2Real airfoil-forecasting setting.

Research question

Do lower field errors imply better physical agreement?

We test whether standard velocity-field scores tell the same story as resolved fluctuation-energy diagnostics, and how frozen forecasts respond when selected parts of the observed flow history are modified.

What the paper is — and is not

A diagnostic protocol, not a new architecture.

The contribution is an evaluation approach for probing model sensitivity and metric disagreement. The experiments do not establish causal physical importance or prove counterfactual physical correctness.

03 / DIAGNOSTIC DESIGN

Change the history. Keep the model frozen.

The same forecasting checkpoint is evaluated before and after a mean-preserving intervention. The model is not retrained; the analysis measures how its forecasts respond to selectively removed temporal fluctuations.

The energetic and random masks cover the same fraction of valid cells, but they do not remove the same amount of fluctuation energy. A larger response therefore indicates greater sensitivity to that input perturbation; it does not independently establish physical importance. The modified histories also have no known physically realizable future target.

05 / INPUT INTERVENTION

Energetic regions consistently influence forecasts.

For the reference surrogate, removing fluctuations from the highest-energy 10% of valid observed cells changes the predicted TKE more than equal-area random masking. The contrast is positive across all 23 trajectory aggregates on both subsets.

What this result supports.

The result supports a repeatable model-sensitivity contrast under the tested equal-area perturbations. It does not isolate spatial importance from the magnitude of fluctuation energy removed, and it should not be described as a causal attribution result.

06 / OUTPUT STRESS TEST

Even a lower error can accompany a larger energy deficit.

A mean-preserving attenuation of predicted fluctuations slightly improves the normalized benchmark errors on the selection subset, yet moves the domain-summed fluctuation-energy ratio farther below one.

This is a diagnostic stress test, not a held-out correction: attenuation gains were selected using both analysis subsets. The example illustrates that sample-normalized errors and a domain-pooled energy ratio can rank predictions differently.

07 / INTERPRETATION

What the evidence does — and does not — establish.

The paper is deliberately bounded. Its results motivate complementary physical diagnostics, while leaving stronger claims about physical importance and generalization for future tests.

01

One physical system. The evidence is from a NACA4418 airfoil benchmark, not a broad cross-system validation.

02

Shared trajectory support. Both analysis subsets use the same 23 experimental trajectories; they do not establish out-of-trajectory generalization.

03

Unequal removed energy. Equal-area masks are not matched for fluctuation energy, so sensitivity and spatial importance cannot be separated.

04

No physical counterfactual target. The modified input histories do not have known physically realizable future observations.

05

Intervention artifacts. Hard mask boundaries may introduce spectral artifacts; smooth and energy-matched controls are needed.

06

Attenuation is exploratory. Its gains were selected on both subsets, so it is not evidence of a held-out physical correction.
