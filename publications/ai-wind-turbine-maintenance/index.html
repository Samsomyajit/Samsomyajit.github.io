AI calibrated framework for adaptive maintenance of wind turbines

An operationally oriented wind-climatology framework for selecting risk-aware maintenance windows, quantifying variability, and characterizing extreme wind-energy regimes at six Indian sites.

View journal paper (DOI) ScienceDirect

Overview

The study evaluates long-term hourly wind measurements from six Indian sites and combines monthly-hourly wind power density analysis with operational maintenance-window selection. It aims to connect seasonal wind characteristics to lower-opportunity-cost, risk-controlled maintenance planning.

Methods at a glance

  • Month–hour wind power density (WPD) heatmaps identify seasonal and diurnal operating patterns.
  • Six-hour sliding-window selection targets lower expected energy loss during planned maintenance.
  • A probabilistic low-wind constraint and bootstrap confidence intervals characterize maintenance reliability and uncertainty.
  • Ramp-rate and volatility diagnostics, plus diurnal wind-regime clustering, describe shorter-term variability.
  • Peaks-over-threshold modeling with a generalized Pareto distribution estimates rare wind-energy extremes.

Interpretation

The analysis provides a reproducible maintenance-planning framework for monsoon-influenced wind regimes. It is based on wind-climatology observations rather than direct component-failure measurements, and should not be described as a validated turbine fault-diagnosis system.
